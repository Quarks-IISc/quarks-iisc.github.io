The 1st semester journey of an IISc undergraduate is a bittersweet one. In the final mile, endsems take over the rigorous schedule of classes, labs, and whatnot. At this crucial juncture, I decided to pause and reflect before fully immersing myself in the “*Yoda*” mode. So, I started looking for a refreshing film, and this film (ZNMD) came out on top of my recommendations. Happy-go-lucky as I am, I was pumped to watch this film. I didn’t know the real surprise was actually waiting for me. 

The film turned out to be a transformative journey for me, awakening my sense of wonder to a different level. 

The story follows three longtime friends who reunite after a long time for a three-week holiday in Spain, probably for the last time as bachelors, before moving on with their lives. The group includes a financial broker, an architect, and a poet, each grappling with personal problems and seeking solutions, though this is not explicitly shown on the surface. On their trip, they encounter numerous people and enriching experiences that never fail to leave a mark on their lives. 

For instance, they undertake an underwater course at sea, a first-time experience for each of them, which leaves them mesmerized by the sheer beauty of marine life and its hidden secrets unfolding right before their eyes. It also transformed one of the friends, who had an aversion to swimming, into confronting his fears and embracing the experience. 

Similarly, we find them trying skydiving, an adventure that requires immense courage and effort; however, the experience was worth it. Jumping from the aircraft into the air and falling towards the earth, they experienced a unique thrill and wonder. All the fear vanished into thin air as they descended together towards the earth, joined hand-in-hand, seeming to have conquered the world. 

Also worth mentioning is the fun and great time they had at the world-famous “La Tomatina” festival, which brought out their inner child, letting them turn back the clock and relive those memories once again. Then there is the scene of them participating in “The Running of the Bulls (*encierro*)” tradition of Spain, which taught them the importance of life and living it to the fullest. They risked their lives after being chased by fighting bulls and steers, from the start till the finish line. It seemed to have reminded them of the attitude of not giving up in the face of adversity. Meanwhile, the broken relationships of the past were healed by the newer ones that they forged during the trip. 

Overall, the camaraderie among the “3 musketeers,” as aptly named during their school days, is a big highlight throughout the movie. The comfort of being with familiar people throughout the trip was beautifully portrayed in the movie, too. The unique experiences they had during the trip strengthened their bond like never before. Moreover, the scenic landscapes and breathtaking views of Spain's cities and countryside made viewers feel as if they were part of the holiday. Lastly, the film’s deepest message is the importance of living in the present, the life that is happening right now, in the little moments that we often overlook.

To conclude, ‘Zindagi Na Milegi Dobara’ is a film that leaves you feeling much lighter, almost as if you've returned from a little holiday yourself, possessing the rare ability to make ordinary life feel more beautiful and full of possibilities. In a nutshell, it’s not just a film that you are going to miss because it made you feel lighter and uplifted, but actually it’s one that’s going to stay with you for as long as you are navigating life and its mysteries.
