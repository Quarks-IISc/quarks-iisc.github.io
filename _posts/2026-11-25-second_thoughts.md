**Date:** 24072020  
**Day:** Monday  
**Time:** 22:00  
   
Dear Diary,  
Life is strange, isn't it? Sometimes, the more we try to figure things out, the more complicated they become.  
   
Today, I had a difficult decision to make. It felt like a choice between my dream and my son's. He had appeared for JEE Advanced and done really well. I wanted him to get into an IIT. After all, every parent wants to see their child succeed, and I thought this was the best thing for his future.  
   
But my son had a different dream.  
   
When he told me about his plans, I got angry. I couldn't understand why he would choose something else when he had such a good opportunity in front of him. So, I raised my voice a little and then walked into my room.  
   
Sitting there alone, I started thinking. Was I right? Was I really thinking about his happiness, or was I just expecting him to do what I wanted?  
   
Somehow, my thoughts went back to my own life.  
   
Ever since I was a child, most decisions had been made for me. My parents decided what was best for me, and after marriage, my husband did the same. I never questioned it. I just went along with whatever life brought my way. Years have passed, and I hardly realized when I stopped thinking about what I wanted for myself.  
   
Then I had my son. Like every mother, I wanted him to have a good life. I wanted him to be successful and have all the things I couldn't have. Slowly, without even realizing it, I started dreaming for him.  
   
But did I ever ask what he wanted for himself?  
That thought hit me hard.  
What am I doing?  
   
I had spent so many years living according to other people's decisions, and now I was expecting my son to do exactly the same. I wanted him to follow the path I had chosen for him, without even thinking what he really wanted.  
   
I was doing to him what had been done to me.  
   
For the first time, I saw it clearly. I didn't want my son to grow up feeling that he had to live up to my  dreams just to make me happy.  
   
No. I couldn't let that happen.  
   
I decided to talk to him. For a moment, I even imagined saying something dramatic like in DDLJ, “Just live your life, Simran\!”  
   
Then I laughed at myself. That would have been too filmy\!  
   
Anyway, I went to him and said, “Listen, from now on, you do what you really want to do. It's your life, and you should be the one making decisions about it. If things go well, I'll be happy for you. And if something goes wrong, don't worry. You'll learn from it and move on. You don't have to live the way I want you to.”  
   
And honestly, I felt lighter after saying it. I also felt proud of him, not because he had done well in an exam, but because he knew what he wanted. He had the courage to make his own choice, and I need to respect that  
   
Looking back, I think sometimes we parents forget that our children are not here to fulfil our dreams. We want to protect them from mistakes, disappointments, and failures while ensuring they have a good future. But in trying to do all that, we may end up deciding their entire lives for them.  
   
Maybe children don't always need us to tell them what to do. Sometimes, they just need us to listen and trust them.  
   
\[Many years have passed since that day.\]  
   
**Another Diary,**  
**Date:** 24082024  
**Day:** Wednesday  
**Time:** 10:00  
   
I am that son, and today, I own a very large company.  
   
Even now, whenever I think about my journey, I remember that day and feel grateful to my mother. I often wonder what would have happened if she had not stopped to think things over. Her decision to support me gave me the freedom to follow my dream and, more importantly, it made me believe that I could make something of my life.  
   
I know it wasn't easy for her to change her mind, but she did, and that means a lot to me even today.  
   
I've realized that misunderstandings often happen when we stop listening to each other. We assume we know what's best for someone, and sometimes we don't even give them a chance to explain themselves. A simple conversation can make such a big difference.  
   
My mother could have stuck to her decision that day. Instead, she gave it a second thought.  
And that changed my life.  
   
Sometimes, all it takes is a second thought to see things differently. And sometimes, that little change in thinking is all we need to give someone the freedom to become who they truly want to be.
