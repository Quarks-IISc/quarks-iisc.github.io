His tired old eyes welled up with tears when he looked at the soft, pink oleander blossoms by the roadside …

The story began about fifty years ago, when he was a young army officer. They were stationed on a little island far away from the mainland to control its ‘wilder’ inhabitants. It was supposed to be a peaceful tribal community that paid taxes to the Empire. However, whispers of a coup had reached the “right” ears. 

The Empire sent a small garrison to quash any ideas of a rebellion. He was part of it too, a little young and inexperienced. 

The men were warned that the people were ‘savages’. They were uncivilized, and they followed strange, barbaric rituals. Truly, a frightening prospect for someone so fresh-faced as he was. But he was determined to face any odds. He dreamt of raking in medals just like the fierce, mustache-twirling generals who ordered them.

It was his third or fourth week on the island when he first saw her. She looked around his age. Every day, she walked past their campsite with her herd of cows and her herding stick.  
She was a ‘low-born woman’; she was not of ‘a refined upbringing’. But she was as brilliant \- as brilliant as (if not more than) most of the grey and balding officials he had seen. She could climb even the steepest trees and was familiar with even the most obscure herbs and shrubs. She even picked up a few phrases in his language from his rants about how he was being overworked every day. 

Every day, close to dusk, he would rush to the fence of their outpost and lean against it, trying desperately to look nonchalant. And she would grace him with the smallest of smiles and a charming tune. This went on for some time. 

By the next harvest festival, the girl began to stop along the way to talk with him, and in a few weeks he began walking alongside her on her way home. She loved making garlands of pink oleanders for the local deity and would often sing songs when working. She told him that the wild ‘prancing’ her people performed around sacrificial fires was a sacred dance and a way to connect with the gods. The colorful face paint they wore was used to show their status in the tribe, not merely as a playful pastime. However, the proud and boastful foreigners never understood her people- when did they even consider them as fellow human beings? 

He was completely smitten with her, and she seemed to show affection for him in her sweet, playful way too. It began sixteen months after they arrived. The Empire had a lot of enemies back home, and one of their rivals had been eyeing the island for some time. The island was an important link in the trade routes. Capturing it meant that all trade ships that passed had to pay tribute to them. 

The battle broke out suddenly. The small garrison was quickly overwhelmed by the sheer number of enemy vessels. He was part of the last line of defense, positioned well inland.   
They fought valiantly but were soon outnumbered. The enemy ruthlessly butchered any dissidents. It was a clear message: *don’t you dare stand against us.*

He somehow managed to escape the makeshift prison that held the Empire’s soldiers, along with a few of his men, and made his way back to his homeland. But her memories… they still haunted him.

For someone who was considered a savage, she had shown him what it meant to be human. It was natural for someone who never understood the culture to call them crude and wild, but he got to know that they had their reasons. 

They worshipped the ocean, the mountain, trees, and grasses because these were their livelihood and needed to be protected. They held festivals to celebrate cattle and treated animals as children because they were compassionate. Only if one could see it all with a broad mind, a fresh pair of eyes, and an open heart… 

He bent down to caress the clustered baby pink blossoms and resumed his walk.
